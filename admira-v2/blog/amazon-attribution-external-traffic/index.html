Amazon Attribution for External Traffic

Amazon Attribution explained: measure external traffic to Amazon listings, tag setup, the Brand Referral Bonus, limitations, and smarter alternatives.

Amazon Attribution is how you measure external traffic to Amazon listings: it is a free console inside Amazon Ads that generates tagged URLs for your Google, Meta, TikTok, email, and influencer campaigns, then reports the clicks, detail-page views, add-to-carts, and purchases each one drives. It is available to sellers in Brand Registry and to vendors. It works, with real limits: measurement is click-based, the lookback is 14 days, and you only see conversions on your own products.

How Amazon Attribution works

Amazon Attribution lives inside the Amazon Ads console. You build a campaign structure that mirrors your external media — typically one order per channel, with ad groups for each campaign or creative — and each ad group produces a unique attribution tag. That tag is simply a tracking-parameter URL pointing at your product listing or Store page.

Any click that arrives through that tag is tied to the shopper's Amazon activity for the next 14 days. Reports then show clicks, detail-page views, add-to-carts, purchases, and sales revenue per tag, so you can finally see whether that TikTok push actually sold units or just generated applause. Before Amazon Attribution existed, off-Amazon spend was almost pure guesswork.

The reporting refreshes daily and lives entirely inside Amazon, so you never touch a pixel on the destination page. That is convenient, but it also means your visibility ends the moment the shopper stops being an Amazon shopper, which is the root of every limitation covered below.

Setting it up without the common mistakes

The tool rewards discipline at setup and punishes shortcuts. Work through these steps in order.

  1. Confirm eligibility. You need Brand Registry as a seller, or a vendor account. Aggregators and resellers without the brand cannot access it, so verify this before you plan campaigns around it.
  2. Mirror your media structure. Create one attribution tag per ad set or creative you want to compare, not a single tag for everything. Granularity you skip at setup is granularity you lose forever, because you cannot retroactively split a blended tag.
  3. Use the bulk tool for scale. Manual tag creation gets painful past a dozen ad groups, so use the bulk-upload feature that exists precisely for this.
  4. Protect the tracking parameters. Send traffic to the exact listing or Store page the tag was built for. Redirect layers and link shorteners that strip parameters will silently zero out your data.

Google Ads has a direct integration that applies tags at scale, which matters because search is usually the largest external traffic source for most brands.

The Brand Referral Bonus changes the math

Sellers enrolled in the Brand Referral Bonus program earn back a bonus averaging around 10% of sales driven through attribution-tagged external traffic, credited against their referral fees. That rebate can turn a marginal external campaign into a profitable one, so enroll before you scale spend rather than after. It is one of the rare cases where Amazon effectively subsidizes your off-Amazon advertising, and it makes accurate tagging worth real money instead of just insight.

What Amazon Attribution will not tell you

Knowing the blind spots keeps you from over-trusting the report or over-cutting a channel it undercounts.

  • No view-through measurement. Only clicks count, so upper-funnel video and awareness campaigns will look far weaker than they truly are.
  • A 14-day window. Considered purchases that close in week three attach to nothing, which penalizes higher-price and higher-consideration products.
  • Halo blindness by default. Reports focus on the promoted products, so a shopper who clicks your tag and buys a different item of yours, or buys after searching your brand later, is easy to miss.
  • No cross-channel deduplication. Amazon Attribution grades external traffic, Amazon Ads grades its own ads, and your DTC analytics grades your site. Nobody referees between them, so summed numbers overstate reality.

Amazon traffic vs your own store

Driving external traffic to Amazon is the better play when your Amazon flywheel matters most: tagged external sales improve organic rank, conversion rates on Amazon tend to be strong, and the Brand Referral Bonus offsets fees. It is the sensible choice during Prime Day pushes or when your DTC site converts poorly and you are effectively paying to send shoppers somewhere they abandon.

Driving traffic to your own store is better when you want the customer relationship: the email address, the remarketing audience, repeat-purchase margin, and clean first-party measurement. Many brands split traffic deliberately and compare cost per new customer on each side. For that comparison to be fair you need measurement that sits above both destinations, whether that is disciplined incrementality testing or a platform like Admira that models your Amazon and DTC channels together instead of leaving them in separate, disagreeing dashboards. FAQ

Is Amazon Attribution free to use?

Yes. Amazon Attribution costs nothing beyond the media you were already buying; it is a reporting console inside Amazon Ads. For sellers enrolled in the Brand Referral Bonus, it actually pays you back a portion of referral fees on the sales your tagged external traffic drives, so measuring can become a small revenue source rather than a cost.

Can I use Amazon Attribution for influencer links?

Yes, and it is one of the strongest use cases. Give each creator a unique attribution tag and you get clicks, detail-page views, and purchase-level reporting for every influencer, which is far more than most influencer campaigns ever produce. Just make sure the creator uses your exact tagged link and does not route it through a shortener that strips the tracking parameters.

Why does Amazon Attribution show fewer sales than my ad platform?

Your ad platforms report modeled and view-through conversions inside their own windows, while Amazon Attribution counts only click-tagged purchases within 14 days. That gap is expected, not a bug. Treat Amazon Attribution as the conservative floor and the platform number as the optimistic ceiling, then triangulate the truth between them with holdouts or MMM.

Does driving external traffic really help organic rank on Amazon?

Amazon has stated that sales velocity influences ranking, and externally driven sales count toward that velocity. So the effect is real, but it is hard to isolate from everything else moving your rank at the same time. If it matters to your strategy, run a geo or time-based test on a subset of ASINs rather than trusting a general claim.
