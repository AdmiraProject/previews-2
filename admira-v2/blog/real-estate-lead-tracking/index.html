Real Estate Lead Tracking: Stages, Speed and Setup

Real estate lead tracking means fixed stages from lead to signed contract, with source and dates. See the stages, metrics and a step-by-step setup.

Real estate lead tracking means recording every lead's source and moving it through fixed stages (new lead, contacted, qualified, site visit, reservation, signed contract) with a date at each step, so you can see where leads stall and which campaigns produce buyers, not just inquiries. For developers and brokers, the two numbers that matter most are how fast each lead gets a first response and what share of leads reach a site visit. This guide defines the stages, the fields to capture, the speed-to-lead rules, and the weekly metrics, with a worked example.

If your main question is how much each sale costs in ad spend, read our companion piece on measuring cost per sale in real estate. This post is about the tracking system that makes that number possible.

What are the lead stages for a real estate developer?

Use stages that describe something that happened, not a feeling about the lead. Each stage needs a written definition and an owner. A common pipeline for new developments:

StageDefinition (entry rule)OwnerTypical date field
1. New leadForm, call, WhatsApp chat or portal inquiry receivedMarketing / systemCreated date
2. ContactedA two-way conversation happened (reply or answered call)Sales advisorFirst contact date
3. QualifiedBudget, timeline and financing fit (credit, cash, mortgage pre-approval)Sales advisorQualified date
4. Site visitVisited the showroom, model unit or construction site (or a live video tour)Sales advisorVisit date
5. ReservationPaid a reservation deposit or signed a reservation agreementSales / adminReservation date
6. SignedPurchase agreement signed and down payment receivedAdmin / legalSigning date
Lost (with reason)No answer after the follow-up cadence, budget, location, bought elsewhereSales advisorLost date + reason

Keep the lost reasons to a short fixed list. "Other" should never be the top reason; if it is, your list is wrong.

What data should you capture for each real estate lead?

  1. Source fields: UTM source, medium, campaign, content and term, plus the ad ID when available. Pass them with hidden fields on forms; see how to pass UTM parameters into your CRM.
  2. Channel of first contact: web form, Meta instant form, WhatsApp, phone call, portal, walk-in.
  3. Project and unit type of interest, so you can compare demand by development.
  4. Stage dates for every stage above, filled automatically when the stage changes.
  5. Assigned advisor and first response timestamp.
  6. Deal value once a reservation happens.

WhatsApp is where tracking breaks most often in LATAM real estate. A lead that clicks a Click-to-WhatsApp ad and chats with an advisor's personal phone never reaches the CRM. Route those chats through the WhatsApp Business Platform connected to the CRM (Kommo, HubSpot, Clientify and others support this), so the ad referral is stored with the contact. Why does speed to lead matter in real estate?

Buyers usually inquire about several projects at once, and the first advisor who answers with useful information sets the comparison. Research published in Harvard Business Review in 2011 (Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads") concluded that most companies do not respond to online leads nearly fast enough. You do not need a benchmark to act on this; measure your own:

  • Median first response time, by advisor and by hour of the day.
  • Share of leads contacted within your target (for example 15 minutes in business hours).
  • Contact rate and visit rate by response-time bucket (under 15 minutes, 15 to 60 minutes, over an hour). This shows whether speed changes results in your market.

Set a written rule: who answers leads at night and on weekends, and what the first message says. An auto-reply that asks one qualifying question is better than silence, but it does not count as "contacted".

Which metrics should a real estate team review weekly?

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Contact rateContacted ÷ new leadsLead quality and team responsiveness
Qualification rateQualified ÷ contactedWhether ads attract the right budget
Visit rateSite visits ÷ qualifiedStrength of the follow-up and the offer
Reservation rateReservations ÷ site visitsSales team and product fit
Cost per visitAd spend ÷ site visitsThe best early proxy for cost per sale
Days lead to reservationReservation date − created dateSales cycle length by source

Cost per visit is the metric to watch while sales are still months away. Signed contracts can take a long time, but visits happen within days or weeks, so they let you judge a new campaign much sooner.

Example: finding the stage where leads stall

Example (hypothetical numbers): a developer spends $10,000 in a month on Meta and Google for one project.

SourceLeadsContactedQualifiedVisitsReservationsCost per visit
Meta instant form ($5,000)40020060122$417
Google Search ($5,000)15012054204$250

Meta brings almost three times more leads, but only half are ever contacted (50% vs 80%), and visits cost 67% more. Two fixes follow from the table: improve the Meta form (add a qualifying question about budget or timeline) and check response time on Meta leads, which often arrive in bursts in the evening. Without stage dates and source fields, the team would only see "550 leads" and argue about quality.

How do you set up real estate lead tracking step by step?

  1. Write the stage definitions above and agree on them with sales.
  2. Configure the pipeline in the CRM with required fields per stage.
  3. Add hidden UTM fields to every form and connect Meta and Google lead forms to the CRM.
  4. Connect WhatsApp Business Platform to the CRM so chats create contacts with the ad referral.
  5. Set response-time alerts and an after-hours rule.
  6. Build a weekly view by source: leads, contact rate, visits, reservations, cost per visit.
  7. Send qualified-lead and visit events back to the ad platforms (Conversions API, offline conversion import) so they optimize for buyers.

Who this is for, and when it doesn't apply

For: developers, brokerages and sales teams that get leads from ads, portals and WhatsApp and close in weeks or months. Less useful: a single agent with a handful of referrals a month; a simple spreadsheet with source and stage is enough. The stage list also needs adapting for rentals, where the cycle is short and "application" replaces "reservation".

FAQ

What is the most important real estate lead metric?

Early on, cost per site visit. Later, cost per signed contract. Lead volume alone is the least reliable.

Which CRM is best for real estate lead tracking?

The one your advisors will actually update. Kommo, HubSpot, Salesforce and Clientify all support pipelines, required fields and WhatsApp integrations.

How do I track leads that come through WhatsApp?

Use the WhatsApp Business Platform connected to your CRM, not personal phones, so each chat creates a contact with its ad source.

How fast should we respond to a real estate lead?

Set a target such as minutes, not hours, in business hours, then check in your own data whether faster responses lead to more visits.

Should a reservation count as a sale?

Track it as its own stage. Reservations can be cancelled, so report reservations and signed contracts separately.

How do I track leads from property portals?

Import portal leads into the CRM with the portal as source. Most portals send email or API notifications that CRMs can parse.
