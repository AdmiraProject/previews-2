Marketing analytics is the practice of collecting, connecting and analyzing data from your marketing channels, website and CRM to understand what drives leads, sales and revenue, and to decide where to invest next. It covers four jobs: describing what happened, diagnosing why, predicting what will happen and recommending what to do. Done well, it answers business questions such as "which campaigns produce customers, and at what cost?" rather than only "how many clicks did we get?"

This guide explains the types of marketing analytics, the data sources behind them, the metrics that matter at each stage of the funnel, and a step-by-step way to set up a process, with a worked example.

What is marketing analytics, and how is it different from reporting?

People often use marketing analytics, reporting, attribution and business intelligence as synonyms. They overlap, but each answers a different question.

DisciplineMain questionTypical output
Marketing reportingWhat happened?Weekly or monthly reports and dashboards
Marketing analyticsWhy did it happen and what should we do?Insights, tests, budget decisions
Marketing attributionWhich touchpoints get credit for a conversion or sale?Revenue and cost per sale by channel, campaign and ad
Marketing mix modeling (MMM)How does each channel contribute over time, including offline?Statistical model of channel contribution
Business intelligence (BI)How is the whole business performing?Company-wide dashboards across finance, sales and operations

Reporting is an input to analytics, not the same thing. A report says cost per lead rose 20%. Analytics finds that the increase came from one audience on Meta and that those leads also close less often, then recommends moving budget. Attribution is one of the main methods inside marketing analytics; our guide on what marketing attribution is covers the models in detail.

What are the four types of marketing analytics?

TypeQuestionMarketing example
DescriptiveWhat happened?We generated 480 leads in October at $21 each.
DiagnosticWhy did it happen?Leads from the new Google campaign closed at half the rate of the brand campaign.
PredictiveWhat is likely to happen?At the current close rate, November spend will produce about 90 sales.
PrescriptiveWhat should we do?Shift 15% of budget from the low-closing campaign to the one with the lowest cost per sale.

Most teams spend almost all their time on descriptive work. The value is in the next three, and they depend on having connected, trustworthy data.

What data does marketing analytics use?

Good marketing analytics combines several sources, because no single tool sees the full journey.

  • Ad platforms (Meta, Google Ads, TikTok, LinkedIn): spend, impressions, clicks and the conversions each platform claims.
  • Web analytics (such as Google Analytics 4): sessions, sources, landing pages and on-site events. GA4 lets standard properties keep user-level and event data for 2 or 14 months, and Google notes that this setting affects explorations and funnel reports, not standard aggregated reports.
  • CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce, Kommo, Clientify): lead status, qualification, deals, revenue and close dates. This is where business results live.
  • Conversations: WhatsApp chats and phone calls, which in Mexico and LATAM are often where the sale actually happens.
  • First-party data: forms, surveys and "how did you hear about us?" answers.

The common failure is analyzing each source alone. Ad platforms tend to claim more conversions than you actually received, because each one credits itself. Analytics only becomes useful for budget decisions when ad data is joined to CRM outcomes.

The top rows are easy to measure and easy to game. The bottom rows are harder but closer to profit. Our article on vanity metrics vs revenue metrics explains why, and our marketing KPI list shows which ones belong on a dashboard.

How do you set up a marketing analytics process?

  1. Write down the decisions you need to make. For example: monthly budget split by channel, which campaigns to pause, which creatives to scale.
  2. Pick one source of truth for revenue. Usually the CRM or the billing system, not the ad platform.
  3. Standardize tracking. Use consistent UTM parameters and capture click IDs and UTMs on every lead form.
  4. Connect ad data to CRM outcomes. Each lead should carry its source, campaign and ad all the way to the closed deal.
  5. Define your metrics once. Agree on what counts as a lead, a qualified lead and a sale, and which date you use.
  6. Build a short dashboard with spend, leads, qualified leads, sales, revenue, cost per sale and ROAS by channel.
  7. Review weekly and act. Every review should end with one or two decisions. Our step-by-step campaign analysis checklist gives a format.
  8. Test causality occasionally. Use holdout or geo tests, or a mix model, to check what attribution cannot prove on its own.

Reporting alone would praise Meta for its lower cost per lead. Analytics shows the opposite: Google Ads produces sales at less than half the cost. The prescriptive step is not "cut Meta". It is to find which Meta campaigns produce the 8 sales, cut the rest, and improve lead qualification. That decision is only possible because leads were tracked through to revenue.

What are the most common marketing analytics mistakes?

  • Adding up platform-reported conversions and treating the total as real.
  • Optimizing for cost per lead without checking lead quality.
  • Changing metric definitions every quarter, so trends break.
  • Building dashboards with 40 charts that nobody reads.
  • Treating attribution as proof of causality. For incremental impact, use experiments or marketing mix modeling.

Who needs a dedicated marketing analytics tool, and who doesn't?

If you run a single channel with a small budget and sell online with one checkout, GA4 plus the ad platform's own reports may be enough. If you sell through a sales team, WhatsApp or long cycles, you need ad data connected to the CRM.

FAQ

What is marketing analytics in simple terms?

It is using data from your ads, website and CRM to learn what brings customers and what does not, so you can spend your budget better.

What is the difference between marketing analytics and web analytics?

Web analytics measures what happens on your website. Marketing analytics includes the website but also ad platforms, CRM, conversations and revenue.

What skills does marketing analytics require?

Basic spreadsheet skills, a clear understanding of the funnel and metric definitions, and the habit of turning numbers into decisions. SQL and statistics help for advanced work but are not required to start.

Is Google Analytics enough for marketing analytics?

It is a good start for traffic and on-site behavior. It does not know which leads became customers unless you connect your CRM data.

How often should I review marketing analytics?

Review campaign performance weekly and budget allocation monthly. Daily checks are useful only for spend pacing and broken tracking.

Is attribution part of marketing analytics?

Yes. Attribution is one of its core methods, used to assign credit for leads and revenue to channels, campaigns and ads.
