Marketing attribution software costs anywhere from about $57 to $4,999+ per month in 2026, and many vendors no longer publish prices at all. Entry-level tools with public pricing sit between roughly $57 and $500 per month; mid-market plans run $700 to $3,500 per month; enterprise contracts are quoted individually. The price is usually tied to one usage metric (website traffic, tracked revenue, ad spend or CRM contacts), so the same tool can cost very different amounts for two companies.

Below is a price table of ten tools we checked on their own pricing pages in November 2026, what actually drives the cost, the hidden costs that don't appear on pricing pages, and a simple way to estimate what you should pay.

What hidden costs should you budget for?

  • Annual contracts. Several vendors bill on 12-month terms. A tool that looks like $1,000/month can be a $12,000 commitment on day one.
  • Onboarding or implementation fees. Some vendors charge separately for setup. Ask whether onboarding is included.
  • Overage charges. Traffic- or revenue-based plans bill extra when you exceed the limit. Admira's Growth plan, for example, adds $15 per additional 1,000 visitors.
  • Internal time. Someone has to clean UTMs, map CRM stages and maintain integrations. Even "no engineering" tools need a few hours a week from marketing ops in the first month.
  • CRM cleanup. If deals aren't updated with revenue and stage, no tool can attribute them. This is the most common reason attribution projects stall.
  • Missing integrations. If your CRM (Kommo, Clientify, a custom build) isn't supported, you may pay for a connector or middleware.

How do you estimate what you should pay?

A simple rule of thumb: attribution software should cost a small fraction of the ad spend it helps you manage, and it should pay for itself with one or two better budget decisions per quarter.

  1. Find your pricing metric. Pull last month's unique visitors, pageviews, tracked revenue and CRM contacts.
  2. Get two or three quotes on the same inputs. Give each vendor the same numbers so the comparison is fair.
  3. Calculate software cost as a share of ad spend. Monthly software cost divided by monthly ad spend.
  4. Estimate the value of one better decision. If you could move 10% of budget from a campaign that doesn't close deals to one that does, what is that worth?
  5. Add hidden costs. Include onboarding, internal hours and contract length.

Example (hypothetical numbers): a B2B company spends $30,000/month on Meta and Google Ads and gets 25,000 unique visitors per month. On Admira's Growth plan ($399/month, up to 30,000 visitors), the tool costs 1.3% of ad spend. If attribution shows that one $5,000/month campaign produces leads but no closed deals, and the team moves that budget to a campaign with a known cost per sale, the tool pays for itself many times over in the first month. Compare that to a $3,500/month plan, which would be 11.7% of the same spend and would need a much bigger win to justify itself.

When is cheaper the right choice, and when isn't it?

A lower-priced tool is usually enough if you run two to four paid channels, sell through a CRM, and mainly need to connect ad spend to closed revenue. That's the core job for most lead-gen and B2B teams. Admira's pricing is built for that case: attribution models, buyer journeys, unlimited dashboards, Conversions API and CRM connection are all on the $199 Starter plan.

Paying more makes sense if you spend six figures per month across many channels, need MMM or formal incrementality testing, require a data warehouse sync, or need security reviews and custom SLAs. Admira is not an MMM consultancy, and for a pure Shopify store with online checkout an ecommerce-first tool like Triple Whale may fit better. FAQ

How much does marketing attribution software cost per month?

Published prices in November 2026 start around $57/month (SegMetrics) and $199/month (Admira), with mid-market plans from roughly $500 to $3,500/month. Many vendors, including Cometly, Hyros and HockeyStack, only quote on a call.

Is there free attribution software?

Triple Whale and Dreamdata offer free plans with limits, and GA4 is free. Free tiers are useful for web analytics but usually don't connect closed CRM revenue to ad spend across platforms.

Why don't more vendors publish prices?

Prices often depend on traffic, revenue or data volume, so vendors prefer to quote. It also lets them bundle services. Ask for the pricing metric and the contract length in writing.

Are attribution tools billed monthly or annually?

Both. Several vendors require annual terms on higher plans and offer discounts of about 10% to 20% (or two months free) for annual prepayment. Check whether monthly billing is available before committing.

What's the cheapest way to start with attribution?

Fix UTMs and pass them into your CRM first; that costs nothing. Then add an entry-level tool that connects ad spend to CRM revenue, and upgrade only when you need more seats, traffic or features.

How do I know if the software is worth it?

Compare the monthly cost to the value of one budget shift it enables. If it can't change at least one decision per quarter, it's too expensive at any price.
