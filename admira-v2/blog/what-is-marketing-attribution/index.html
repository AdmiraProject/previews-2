What Is Marketing Attribution? Models, Data and Examples

Marketing attribution is the practice of connecting each sale or qualified lead back to the marketing touchpoints that produced it (ads, searches, emails, WhatsApp chats, sales calls) and assigning credit to each one, so you know which channels, campaigns and creatives actually generate revenue. Ad platforms each report their own conversions, so the totals rarely match your CRM. Attribution replaces those competing claims with one record per customer: where they came from, what they touched, and what they paid.

This guide covers how attribution works, the main models, the data you need, a worked example with numbers, and a practical way to start in about two weeks.

What does marketing attribution actually measure?

Attribution answers three questions for every closed deal or purchase:

  • Source: which channel and campaign brought the person in first (for example, a Meta lead ad or a Google search).
  • Path: which touchpoints happened between the first visit and the sale (retargeting, email, a WhatsApp conversation, a demo).
  • Value: how much revenue the customer produced, and how much of that revenue each touchpoint gets credit for.

When you add ad spend to that record, you can calculate the numbers leadership cares about: cost per sale, customer acquisition cost (CAC), return on ad spend (ROAS) based on real revenue, and payback time. Without attribution, most teams stop at cost per lead, which says nothing about whether those leads ever bought.

Why don't Meta, Google and my CRM agree?

Each ad platform counts conversions inside its own walls, with its own attribution window. Meta's widely used default is 7-day click and 1-day view, though Meta has adjusted its window options, so check the setting on each ad set. Google Ads uses its own conversion windows and, by default, data-driven attribution. Both can claim the same sale. Your CRM, meanwhile, only knows what your sales team recorded.

The result is that the sum of platform-reported conversions is often higher than the sales you actually closed. We cover the mechanics in why Meta, Google Ads and GA4 conversion numbers don't match and why platform ROAS can be inflated. Attribution software exists to deduplicate those claims against one source of truth: the revenue in your CRM or checkout.

What are the main attribution models?

An attribution model is the rule that decides how credit is split across touchpoints. No model is "true"; each one answers a slightly different question.

ModelHow credit is splitBest forMain blind spot
First touch100% to the first interactionUnderstanding what creates demandIgnores everything that closed the deal
Last touch (last click)100% to the final interaction before conversionSimple reporting, short buying cyclesOvercredits branded search and retargeting
LinearEqual credit to every touchpointSeeing the full journey without bias to one endTreats a passing impression like a sales call
Time decayMore credit to touchpoints closer to the saleLonger cycles with clear closing activityUndervalues the channel that started the journey
U-shaped (position-based)Typically 40% first, 40% lead creation, 20% split in betweenLead-gen and B2B where lead creation is a milestonePercentages are a convention, not a measurement
Data-driven / algorithmicCredit estimated from patterns in converting vs non-converting pathsHigh volume of conversionsHard to audit; needs a lot of data

Note that Google Analytics 4 removed first click, linear, time decay and position-based models in November 2023, leaving data-driven and last click options (Google Analytics Help). If you want to compare models side by side, you need a dedicated tool. For a deeper comparison, read multi-touch attribution models explained and what last-click attribution hides.

What does attribution look like with real numbers?

Example (hypothetical numbers): a B2B services company closes a $12,000 deal. The buyer's journey had four touchpoints:

  1. Clicked a Meta video ad and visited the site.
  2. Came back through a Google search for the brand name.
  3. Clicked a Click-to-WhatsApp ad and chatted with a sales rep.
  4. Attended a demo call booked by the rep, then signed.
TouchpointFirst touchLast touchLinearU-shaped
Meta video ad$12,000$0$3,000$4,800
Google brand search$0$0$3,000$1,200
Click-to-WhatsApp ad (lead created)$0$0$3,000$4,800
Demo call (sales)$0$12,000$3,000$1,200

Under last touch, the ads look worthless and the team might cut the Meta budget. Under first touch, WhatsApp looks worthless. The U-shaped view shows that the Meta ad started the journey and the WhatsApp ad turned interest into a lead. If Meta spend for that month was $6,000 and it touched three closed deals like this one, the Meta campaign's revenue-based ROAS is very different from the "cost per lead" its dashboard shows.

What data do you need for attribution to work?

Attribution is only as good as the data it joins. You need four inputs:

InputWhat it isCommon failure
Touchpoint dataClicks, visits, UTMs, click IDs (fbclid, gclid, ttclid), WhatsApp ad referralsInconsistent or missing UTMs
IdentityEmail, phone or a first-party ID that links a visit to a leadForms that don't pass tracking data into the CRM
OutcomesLead stages, deals won, revenue, refunds from the CRM or checkoutDeals closed offline and never updated
CostSpend by campaign, ad set and ad from each platformSpend tracked in spreadsheets, not joined to revenue

Privacy changes also matter: browsers and iOS limit third-party cookies and cross-app tracking, which is why first-party and server-side data collection have become the default. How is attribution different from MMM and incrementality?

Attribution is one of three measurement methods. They answer different questions and work best together.

  • Attribution (multi-touch): tracks individual journeys. Good for daily and weekly decisions by campaign and creative. Weak on channels you can't click, like TV or podcasts.
  • Marketing mix modeling (MMM): uses aggregated spend and sales over time to estimate each channel's contribution. Good for annual budget splits. Needs months or years of history.
  • Incrementality testing: holdout or geo experiments that measure what would have happened without a channel. The closest thing to causal proof, but each test takes weeks.

How do you start with marketing attribution?

You don't need a data team. A realistic first rollout looks like this:

  1. Define the outcome. Pick the event that means money: a closed-won deal, a paid order or a funded loan. Not a form fill.
  2. Clean your UTMs. Agree on one naming convention for source, medium, campaign and content, and apply it to every live ad.
  3. Capture tracking data on every lead. Pass UTMs and click IDs into the CRM through hidden form fields or an integration, including WhatsApp leads.
  4. Connect ad spend. Pull cost from Meta, Google Ads and TikTok at campaign and ad level.
  5. Join it all to CRM revenue. Match each closed deal to its journey and compare two or three models side by side.
  6. Send results back to the platforms. Use the Conversions API so Meta and Google optimize for qualified leads and sales, not just form fills (how to connect offline conversions).
  7. Review weekly. Look at cost per sale and revenue by campaign every week, and sanity-check totals against blended metrics like MER.

Who needs attribution software, and who doesn't?

It's a good fit if:

  • You spend on two or more paid channels and the platforms' numbers don't add up.
  • You sell through a CRM, a sales team or WhatsApp, so the sale happens days or weeks after the click.
  • Leadership asks for CAC or ROI by channel and you build it by hand in spreadsheets.

It may not be worth it yet if:

  • You run a single channel with a small budget; the platform's own reporting is probably enough.
  • You have very few sales per month; no model can find patterns in a handful of deals, so focus on clean CRM data first.
  • You mainly need TV, radio or out-of-home measurement; that is MMM territory.

FAQ

What is marketing attribution in simple terms?

It's a way to see which marketing touchpoints led to each sale and how much credit each one deserves. It turns "we got 300 leads" into "this campaign produced $40,000 in closed revenue."

Which attribution model is best?

There's no single best model. Lead-gen and B2B teams often start with U-shaped or linear alongside last touch, then compare. The value is in comparing models, not picking one forever.

Is Google Analytics 4 enough for attribution?

GA4 is useful for web behavior, but it only offers data-driven and last click models and doesn't know which leads became revenue in your CRM. Teams with offline or sales-led conversions usually need a CRM-connected tool.

How long does it take to set up attribution?

The tracking itself can be live the same day. Getting trustworthy reports usually takes one to three weeks, because you need clean UTMs, connected spend and enough closed deals to compare.

Does attribution still work without cookies?

Yes. Modern tools use first-party data, server-side tracking and CRM matching instead of third-party cookies. Coverage is never 100%, so validate with blended metrics and occasional incrementality tests.

What's the difference between attribution and tracking?

Tracking collects the data (clicks, visits, leads). Attribution interprets it, assigning revenue credit to touchpoints so you can make budget decisions.
