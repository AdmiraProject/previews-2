The best attribution software for law firms in 2027 combines three things: call tracking, form and chat tracking, and an intake or CRM system that records which leads became signed cases and fees. CallRail and WhatConverts cover calls and forms from $30 to $95 a month, Lead Docket, Lawmatics and Clio Grow report lead sources inside intake, Ruler Analytics and HubSpot tie sources to revenue, and Admira fits firms that get clients through WhatsApp and paid social. Prices were checked in November 2026 on each vendor's page, and the list is updated for 2027 planning.

This guide is published by Admira, a marketing attribution platform. We include Admira only where it fits and state its limits. Nothing here is legal advice: attorney advertising and call-recording rules vary by state and country, so check yours with your bar or counsel.

How we chose these tools

A law firm's real conversion is a signed engagement, not a call. So we looked for tools that either track the first contact (call, form, chat, WhatsApp) or record what happened after it (consultation, signed, fee). We checked each vendor's pricing page in November 2026, noted what is published and what is quote-only, and read product pages for source reporting and ROI features. We did not use review-site rankings or vendor case-study numbers. Clio's pricing page blocked automated access, so we cite only its product page.

CallRail: best for call-driven practices

CallRail's Lead Tracking plan starts at $50/month with call and text tracking, recording, routing and transcription, including 5 local numbers and 250 minutes. Lead Tracking Complete at $95/month adds form tracking and multi-touch cost-per-lead reporting. Conversation intelligence plans start at $150/month. Extra numbers, minutes and texts are billed as usage (CallRail pricing).

Pros: mature dynamic number insertion, Google Ads integration. Limits: it knows the call, not the fee; you still need intake data. Our phone call attribution guide covers the setup.

WhatConverts: best low-cost all-in-one lead tracking

WhatConverts starts at $30/month for call tracking and $60/month (Plus) for calls, forms and chat, with Elite at $160/month adding customer journey and multi-click attribution. Agency plans start at $500/month for unlimited accounts, and white label costs $50/month extra. Pro and above list HIPAA compliance (WhatConverts pricing).

Pros: cheap entry, lets you mark leads as quotable and add sale values. Limits: manual value entry unless you connect a CRM.

Lead Docket: best for intake teams

Lead Docket captures web form leads, triggers follow-up emails and tasks, uses AI to assess case viability, and syncs to Filevine. Its reporting shows which lead sources and intake strategies bring in the most clients and revenue (Lead Docket). Its homepage does not list prices; third-party listings cite figures that we could not confirm.

Pros: built for case intake and source reporting. Limits: attribution is only as good as the source captured at intake.

Lawmatics: best intake plus marketing suite

Lawmatics offers intake, custom forms, appointment booking and source reporting on all plans. URL UTM tracking, analytics dashboards, ROI tracking and custom reports start on Premium. All plans are custom-quoted (Lawmatics pricing).

Pros: ROI by source inside the same tool that signs clients. Limits: no public prices, and the entry plan lacks UTM tracking.

Clio Grow: best if you already use Clio

Clio Grow captures inquiries from web, email, phone, text, chat and forms, runs conflict checks, books consultations, and shows each lead's source next to its history. Clio says it can show which channels bring in the most revenue but does not detail how (Clio Grow). Pricing is not shown on the product page.

Pros: one system from intake to matter. Limits: ad-level attribution is not described publicly.

Ruler Analytics: best for revenue attribution in the UK and EU

Ruler's Small plan costs £299 ($400) a month for up to 10,000 visits, with multi-touch attribution, CRM integrations (Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics 365, Pipedrive) and opportunity and revenue stage reporting. It lists dynamic call tracking among its tracking features (Ruler pricing).

Pros: closes the loop to CRM revenue. Limits: priced above call-only tools.

HubSpot: best if your firm already runs marketing in HubSpot

HubSpot's attribution reports need Marketing Hub Professional or Enterprise. Professional starts at $800/month on annual billing plus a $3,000 onboarding fee. It works well when intake staff update deal stages; it does not track calls on its own.

Admira: best for WhatsApp and paid social intake

Admira ties Meta, Google Ads, TikTok and Click-to-WhatsApp leads to CRM revenue, and it connects to HubSpot, Salesforce, Kommo, Clientify and ActiveCampaign. It fits firms in Mexico, LATAM or the US Hispanic market where potential clients message on WhatsApp instead of calling. Plans start at $199/month; Growth is $399 (Admira pricing). Limits: call tracking numbers are not among its listed features, so call-heavy firms should pair its lead tracking with call data from CallRail or WhatConverts. It is also a young brand with few public reviews. See WhatsApp ads attribution.

How do you set up attribution for a law firm?

  1. List every intake channel: calls, forms, chat, WhatsApp, referrals, walk-ins.
  2. Add tracking numbers by source and capture UTMs in hidden form fields (see how to pass UTMs into your CRM).
  3. Define stages in intake: lead, qualified, consultation, signed, closed with fee.
  4. Make the source field required before a lead can move to "signed".
  5. Record the expected or actual fee on signed matters.
  6. Send signed cases back to Google Ads and Meta as offline conversions so bidding learns from cases, not calls (see how to import offline conversions into Google Ads).
  7. Review cost per signed case by channel each month.

What about advertising and privacy rules?

In the US, most state bars base their advertising rules on the ABA Model Rules, which prohibit false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services (ABA Model Rule 7.1). Call recording consent rules also differ by state. Attribution data can contain sensitive case details, so limit who sees intake notes and avoid sending case details to ad platforms; send only the conversion event. This is general information, not legal advice.

Who this list is for, and who it is not for

For: firms spending on Google, Meta or local ads that want cost per signed case by channel. Not for: referral-only practices with no paid marketing, or firms that do not record intake outcomes. Without outcomes, any tool can only count calls.

FAQ

What is the best call tracking software for law firms?

CallRail and WhatConverts are the most common choices, starting at $50 and $30 a month respectively (checked November 2026). Both add form tracking on higher plans.

Can legal intake software track marketing ROI?

Yes, partly. Lawmatics (Premium and up), Lead Docket and Clio Grow report lead sources, but they rely on the source being captured correctly at intake.

What should a law firm measure instead of cost per lead?

Cost per signed case and fee revenue per channel. Leads vary widely in case value and viability.

Do I need a separate attribution tool if I have intake software?

Often you need call tracking plus intake. A separate attribution tool helps when you run several paid channels and need ad-level data tied to signed cases.

Is it safe to send law firm conversions to Google and Meta?

Send only the conversion event and hashed identifiers the platform requires, never case details. Check your bar's rules and your privacy obligations first.

Does Admira track phone calls?

Call tracking numbers are not among Admira's listed features. Firms that depend on calls should pair it with a call tracking tool.
