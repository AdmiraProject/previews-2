The best attribution software for ecommerce in 2027 depends on your store's size and sales channel: Triple Whale suits most Shopify brands that want an all-in-one dashboard, Northbeam fits DTC brands with large media budgets, Polar Analytics suits teams that want their own data warehouse, Rockerbox fits enterprise brands that need MTA plus MMM and incrementality, Wicked Reports and Lifetimely fit subscription and LTV-focused stores, and GA4 is the free baseline. Prices below were checked in November 2026 on each vendor's own page, and the list is updated for 2027 planning.

This guide is published by Admira, a marketing attribution platform. We include Admira only for the stores where it fits (brands that sell through WhatsApp, sales reps or a CRM), and we say plainly that a pure Shopify store with online checkout is usually better served by an ecommerce-first tool.

How we chose these tools

We looked at tools that ecommerce teams actually shortlist for attribution, then checked each vendor's pricing page in November 2026. We scored them on five things: how they capture data (pixel, server-side, integrations), which attribution models they offer, whether they cover profit and LTV, how transparent the pricing is, and how long setup takes. We did not run paid tests of every tool, and we did not use review-site rankings. Where a vendor does not publish a price, we say "not published" instead of guessing. Triple Whale's pricing page blocked automated access, so its plan details come from a third-party review dated November 2026 and should be confirmed with the vendor.

Triple Whale: best for most Shopify brands

Triple Whale combines a pixel, dashboards and an AI assistant (Moby) for Shopify-first brands. It has a free plan with first and last click attribution; multi-touch attribution, custom dashboards and CSV export sit on paid plans. Paid plans (Foundation, Automate, Enterprise) are priced on order revenue over the last 365 days, with unlimited users, and run as 12-month subscriptions (third-party pricing review, Nov 2026).

Pros: fast setup for Shopify, broad ecommerce dashboards. Limits: no published paid prices, annual commitment. Compare options in our Triple Whale alternatives guide.

Northbeam: best for DTC brands with large budgets

Northbeam offers independent first-party multi-touch attribution, view-through attribution with deterministic views, and optional incrementality and MMM Plus add-ons on higher plans. Starter is listed at $1,500 for brands spending under $1.5M a year on ads, and Professional at $3,500/month for up to $500K/month in spend (Northbeam pricing).

Pros: strong modeling, works beyond Shopify on Professional. Limits: cost is hard to justify below a few hundred thousand dollars in yearly ad spend. See Northbeam alternatives, or our head-to-head of Triple Whale vs Northbeam vs Admira.

Polar Analytics: best for a dedicated ecommerce warehouse

Polar bundles business intelligence, multi-touch attribution and a first-party pixel on its Core plan. Its Custom plan adds incrementality lift tests on Meta, Google, TikTok or TV with a data scientist. Pricing is set by annual GMV and shown only through a demo (Polar pricing).

Pros: owns the data layer, good for teams that want BI and attribution together. Limits: no public price, so budget planning needs a call.

Rockerbox: best for enterprise measurement

Rockerbox combines multi-touch attribution, marketing mix modeling and incrementality testing on one data foundation, with warehouse delivery to BigQuery, Redshift and Snowflake. It says tests can launch in 1 to 2 weeks while MTA or MMM typically takes 6 to 8 weeks (Rockerbox). Pricing is not published.

Pros: covers offline and hard-to-track channels. Limits: longer setup, sized for larger brands.

Wicked Reports: best for subscription brands

Wicked Reports focuses on new versus repeat customers, cohorts and LTV with lifetime lookback. Measure costs $499/month for brands up to $2.5M in annual revenue, Scale $699 and Maximize $999, which includes Advanced Signal (Meta CAPI) and 5 Forces AI (Wicked Reports pricing).

Pros: rebill and LTV attribution. Limits: high entry for small stores.

Lifetimely (AMP): best for profit and LTV on a budget

Lifetimely is free up to 50 orders a month; the M plan costs $149/month for up to 3,000 orders, XL $499 and XXL $749. Attribution is listed on every paid plan, and an Amazon add-on costs $75/month (AMP pricing).

Pros: clear order-based pricing, P&L and LTV. Limits: lighter on cross-channel modeling than Northbeam or Rockerbox.

Google Analytics 4: the free baseline

GA4 is free and should run on every store. It offers data-driven and last click attribution and reads Shopify purchases through Google's integrations. It is a good sanity check, but it reports sessions and modeled credit, not a full revenue model by ad. Treat it as a baseline, not the final word on budget calls.

Admira: best when sales close outside checkout

Admira ties Meta, Google Ads, TikTok and Click-to-WhatsApp leads to CRM revenue. For ecommerce it fits stores that sell high-ticket or custom products through WhatsApp chats or sales reps, where the "order" is a closed deal in Kommo, HubSpot or another CRM. Plans start at $199/month (up to 10,000 unique visitors), Growth is $399 and Custom $1,500 (Admira pricing). Limits: it is a young brand with few public reviews, and for a pure Shopify store with online checkout, Triple Whale or a similar ecommerce tool usually fits better. How do you evaluate a tool in a trial?

  1. Pick one month of orders you already know well.
  2. Connect your store and ad accounts and let the tool backfill that month.
  3. Compare total attributed revenue against Shopify sales (it should not exceed them).
  4. Check new versus returning customer splits by channel.
  5. Compare each channel's credit against Meta and Google's own reports; expect platforms to claim more. Our post on platform ROAS inflation explains why.
  6. Decide on one number per channel your team will use weekly.

Who this list is for, and who it is not for

For: Shopify and DTC brands, subscription stores, and ecommerce teams spending enough on paid media that misattribution costs real money. Not for: stores spending under a few thousand dollars a month on ads (GA4 and platform reports are enough), Amazon-only sellers, and teams that need a full MMM study more than daily attribution.

FAQ

What is the best attribution software for Shopify?

For most Shopify brands, Triple Whale or Polar is the usual starting point, with Lifetimely as the budget option. Brands with large budgets often move to Northbeam.

Is GA4 enough for ecommerce attribution?

GA4 is enough for small stores and as a free check. As ad spend grows, a dedicated tool adds server-side data, new versus returning splits and creative-level views.

How much does ecommerce attribution software cost?

Published prices checked in November 2026 run from free (GA4, Lifetimely up to 50 orders) to $149/month (Lifetimely M), $499 (Wicked Reports) and $1,500 listed for Northbeam Starter. Triple Whale paid plans, Polar and Rockerbox are quoted.

Do I still need the Meta Conversions API?

Yes. Attribution tools measure; the Conversions API sends better data back to Meta for optimization. See our Meta Conversions API guide.

Can attribution tools prove incrementality?

Not on their own. Multi-touch models assign credit; incrementality needs holdout or geo tests. Polar, Northbeam and Rockerbox offer test options.

When is Admira a fit for an ecommerce brand?

When orders close through WhatsApp, a sales rep or a CRM rather than an online cart. For pure Shopify checkout, an ecommerce-first tool usually fits better.
